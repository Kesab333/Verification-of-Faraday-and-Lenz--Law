Move the semicircular frame so its bar magnet passes the stationary copper coil; the changing magnetic flux induces the voltage shown on the digital voltmeter.

Diagram

Fig. 1: Circuit diagram for the determination of voltage induced in the coil due to the magnet passing through it.

Calculation

Measure the peak EMF for each trial and compare it with the magnet's angular velocity.

Observation: peak EMF increases as the magnet passes the equilibrium position faster.

Energy: electrical energy produced in the coil is dissipated as heat, reducing the mechanical amplitude.

Experiments

Try different conditions: vary the coil turns, magnet strength and starting angle, then compare the peak EMF readings.

Use Reset between trials so each experiment starts from the same position.

Observation

When the magnet moves toward or away from the coil, the magnetic flux changes and the meter registers an induced EMF.

The polarity reverses as the motion reverses. The EMF falls to nearly zero at each turning point because the magnet is momentarily stationary.
